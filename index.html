Crew of 4 — How the Diamond Fits Together

Four officials cover the whole field by dividing responsibility between the middle of the formation and the two sidelines:

  • Referee (R) — starts on the throwing-arm side of the QB, 12–14 yards deep and wide. Owns the QB, the passer, and action directly behind the line.
  • Umpire (U) — 5–10 yards deep in the defensive backfield, opposite the R's side. Owns the interior line, the five ineligibles, and the point of attack.
  • Head Linesman (H) — straddles the neutral zone on the sideline opposite the press box. Owns the chain crew, the line-to-gain, and pre-snap fouls on their side.
  • Line Judge (L) — straddles the neutral zone on the press-box sideline. Mirrors H's duties on the opposite side; also times intermissions and coordinates media/TV timeouts.

Every infraction name below is a link — click it to jump to its full definition in the Glossary at the bottom of this guide.

What "Your Zone" Actually Means

The manual uses this loosely, so here's what it actually maps to in each context:

  • Sideline responsibility (H/L): your own sideline, goal line to goal line – end line to end line, the full length of the field on your side.
  • Pass coverage (H/L): the side of the field the play is developing toward, relative to where you're standing.
  • "Side Zone" (rule-defined, NCAA Definitions Sec. 25, Art. 6): the strip of field between the hash marks and the near sideline – this one has an exact rule definition, unlike the general "your zone" language.

Kickoff Formation – Restraining Lines, 9-Yard Marks & Restricted Area

  • 9-Yard Marks: short marks 9 yards in from each sideline, running the length of the field. On a kickoff, every Team A player (except the kicker) must be back between the 9-yard marks after the ready-for-play signal – they can't be lined up out near the sideline beyond that mark.
  • Restricted Area: the sideline zone right around the restraining lines that must be kept clear of bench personnel, so the wing officials can do their restraining-line job unobstructed and players have room to work.
  • Restraining Lines: the kicking team's line (where the ball is teed up) and the receiving team's line, exactly 10 yards downfield of it – neither team may cross their line until the ball is kicked.

Communication & Signals Between Officials

Full detail on every signal is in the Signal Glossary at the bottom of this guide – click any signal name below to jump straight to it.

  • Player Count Signal – closed fist = 11 players (correct), open palm = fewer than 11. R+U count Team A, H+L count Team B on scrimmage downs; on kickoffs, U+H count the kicking team, R+L count the receiving team.
  • Substitution Signal – both arms, closed fists, extended horizontal. Given by H/L when Team A subs, and by R to U when Team B is expected to match.
  • Release Signal – R drops both arms and points at U once substitutions are done.
  • Punch Back – signals a pass went backward (live ball), not incomplete (dead ball). Used most on quick screens near the line.
Handy O2O (Official-to-Official Radio) Shorthand
  • "Subs" (Team A substituting) → "Match" (Team B responding)
  • "Touched behind" / "touched beyond" – where a pass was touched relative to the neutral zone
  • "Number [X] is in" – a back is stationary in the tackle box (relevant to blocking-below-the-waist legality)
  • "Five will get you one" – line to gain is less than 5 yards away
  • "Position 1–5" – sideline official's spot before a try or after a touchback
  • "Under two minutes" / "under one minute" – clock milestones
  • "Coach accepts" / "Coach declines" – penalty enforcement decision relayed from the sideline
  • "Clock is hot" / "clock on the snap" – whether the game clock starts on the ready-for-play signal or the snap

Multiple flags or a genuinely unusual situation are never O2O conversations – come together face to face.

Roughing / Running Into the Kicker or Holder
  • Contact is a live-ball personal foul that endangers the kicker/holder? → Roughing. 15 yds, previous spot, automatic 1st down.
  • Kicker/holder is only displaced, not endangered? → Running Into. 5 yds, previous spot. No auto 1st down.
  • Contact happened only because of a legal or illegal block by the opponent, or the defender had already touched the kick? → No foul.
  • When in question whether it's Running Into or Roughing → Call Roughing.
Kick-Catch Interference
  • Foul happened between the goal lines (not a free-kick foul behind B's 25)? → Receiving team's ball, 1st down, 15 yds beyond the spot of the foul.
  • Foul on a free kick, behind B's 25, against a player who gave a valid fair-catch signal? → Penalize from the B-25.
  • Foul happened behind the goal line? → Touchback – penalized from the succeeding spot (20-yd line on a punt, 25-yd line on a free kick).
  • Contact also meets the criteria for targeting or another personal foul? → Team B's choice: 15 yds at the spot the dead ball belongs to B, or at the spot of the foul.
  • Flagrant contact → Ejection/disqualification, regardless of which branch above applies.

Level-of-play enforcement guidance for each foul is inside its Glossary entry, not in this table — enforcement yardage never changes by level, only how quickly the foul gets flagged does.

Referee (R)

Pregame
  • Arrive on the field with the crew, no later than 15 minutes before kickoff.
  • Inspect the entire field with L – unusual markings, hazards, irregularities.
  • Arrange with the home-sideline captain to meet at midfield 4 minutes before kickoff.
  • Meet with both head coaches beforehand: confirm rules-adherent expectations, timeout process, TV timeouts if applicable.

Coin Toss
  • Escort captains from your sideline to the center of the field, 3 minutes before kickoff.
  • Introduce captains to each other; advise of any ground rules/irregularities.
  • Backs to the sidelines. Visiting captain calls the toss in the air.
  • Indicate the winner by hand on shoulder; present options in order (defer allowed to 2nd half).
  • Signal choice to the press box; record results with U at midfield.

Kickoff (Free Kicks)
  • Position: goal line, opposite the press box (see the Kickoff Formation diagram on the Overview tab).
  • Obtain ready signals from the entire crew; sound whistle and give ready-for-play.
  • Count Team B (the receiving team) – on free kicks, R and L count the receiving team while U and H count the kicking team.
  • Deep kicks: responsible for the goal line/pylon on your sideline; watch for Offside and illegal wedge formations.
  • Short/onside kicks: think onside first, then anticipate a return for a TD.
  • Watch for legal/illegal touching, fair-catch signals, and interference with the opportunity to catch.

Before the Snap (all scrimmage downs)
  • Position deep and wide on the QB's throwing-arm side, 12–14 yards deep and wide.
  • Check the offensive formation, count Team A, signal the count to U – closed fist for 11, open palm for fewer.
  • Watch setbacks and the QB for illegal motion; assist U on restricted-lineman movement.

Substitutions & Player Counts
  • You and U own the correct count on Team A, every down – see the Player Count Signal.
  • If Team A substitutes, hold the snap until Team B has a real chance to match.
  • If Team B is clearly about to respond with subs, give U the Substitution Signal – that's U's cue to cover the ball.
  • Release U once both teams finish substituting – see the Release Signal.
  • Don't let the offense rush the snap specifically to deny the defense a chance to match – that's Illegal Substitution/Delay of Game on Team A instead.
  • Watch for a trick play built around hiding a substitute near the bench – that's also Illegal Substitution.

Running Plays
  • Help with the tackle on your side immediately after the snap.
  • Watch action behind the line and the runner to the neutral zone; watch the QB on hand-offs/backward passes.
  • Take the runner behind the neutral zone until dead; coordinate sideline coverage with H/L.
  • On an option, stay with the QB until no threat of a foul, then trail into the side zone.
  • Watch for late hits and fouls after the ball is dead – you're the only one who signals a dead-ball foul [S7].

Pass Plays
  • Observe the near tackle at the snap; switch to the far tackle once the QB sets up to pass.
  • When you read pass, back out at a 45° angle, staying wide and deep to keep the play in front of you.
  • Protect the passer from Roughing the Passer – no rough treatment, no forcible head/neck contact.
  • Verbally alert defenders once the passer has released the ball.
  • Rule on the legality of the forward pass (thrown from beyond the neutral zone, or out of bounds on a run/pass option).
  • Rule on Intentional Grounding – mark the spot with your bean bag and confirm eligible-receiver positioning with the crew.

Scrimmage Kicks (Punts)
  • Position slightly behind and to the L side of the kicker, watching the snap and flight to the kicker.
  • Rule on contact with the kicker – Roughing/Running Into the Kicker (see Overview flowchart).
  • Signal if Team A is not in a legal scrimmage kick formation (open hand on chest).
  • Watch the kick's flight; if headed toward a sideline, direct the covering official to the out-of-bounds spot.

Try & Field Goal
  • Snapped on/outside B-15: position 10 yds wide of the kicker, facing the holder.
  • Snapped inside B-15: position ~5 yds behind the holder, in line with the tee and goal posts.
  • Rule on the kick passing between the uprights (you have sole responsibility for the uprights – L rules on the crossbar).
  • Signal score only when positive all requirements are met – never back up a teammate's TD signal.

Goal Line
  • Same coverage as any short-yardage down – forward progress is usually determined by the sideline official.
  • Do not back up other officials' TD signals.

Timeouts & Two-Minute
  • Signal a full charged timeout facing the press box, pointing toward the requesting team.
  • Only R may stop the clock for a coach's conference (Referee's Timeout).
  • Varsity: stop the clock for the Two-Minute Timeout at 2:00 in the 2nd/4th when the ball is dead; announce "Two-minute timeout" to the press box.
  • Sub-Varsity: no Two-Minute Timeout stoppage – but the under-2:00 timing rules (10-second runoff, etc.) still apply once a down is snapped under 2:00.

Close of Period / Half
  • Sound whistle at time expiration only if the ball is dead; signal end of period with ball held overhead [S14].
  • You are primarily responsible for determining the end of any period.
  • At the end of the 2nd period, give the start-the-clock signal for halftime once the field is clear.

Postgame
  • Leave the field together with the crew – neither seek nor avoid coaches.
  • Complete foul reports; obtain incident-report input from the crew if needed.
  • Any ejection, fight, targeting foul, or 15-yard sideline penalty requires an incident report within 24 hours.

Umpire (U)

Pregame
  • Spot check player equipment – pads, braces, pants, shirts, tape – with team trainers.
  • Arrange to meet the home-sideline captain with R, 4 minutes before kickoff.

Coin Toss
  • Introduce your captain(s) to R; remain with R to witness the toss and verify choices.

Kickoff (Free Kicks)
  • Position: start center field, then move to the kicking team's restraining line, press-box side (see the Kickoff Formation diagram on the Overview tab).
  • Count Team A players and instruct the kicker.
  • Confirm 9-yard-mark compliance and that only the kicker is outside 5 yards of the restraining line.
  • Be alert for Team A Offside; watch for illegal action on the kicker.
  • Watch for correct player counts on each side of the kicker once kicked.

Before the Snap (all scrimmage downs)
  • Position 5–10 yards deep, opposite R's side, avoiding linebacker sightlines.
  • Know the five ineligibles (numbers 50–79); ensure at least five are present.
  • Count Team A, signal to R – closed fist for 11, open palm for fewer; rule on illegal movement by restricted linemen before the snap.

Substitutions & Player Counts
  • You and R own the correct count on Team A, every down – see the Player Count Signal.
  • When R gives you the Substitution Signal, cover the ball (or stand near the snapper) to prevent the snap.
  • Hold that position until R gives the Release Signal.
  • Ensure five linemen numbered 50–79 are present and on the line (except in scrimmage-kick formation) before you release the ball.

Running Plays
  • Key the snapper and two guards; help with tackles if an obvious foul is visible.
  • Check for Holding and illegal use of hands by both sides.
  • Cover the point of attack first, then behind the ball, then around the runner.

Pass Plays
  • Observe ineligible interior linemen and the legality of their numbers.
  • When you read pass, move toward the neutral zone; know whether the pass crosses it.
  • Assist on trapped-pass rulings; know the ball's lateral position if incomplete.
  • Primary responsibility for legal pass when snapped on/inside B's 5-yard line.

Scrimmage Kicks (Punts)
  • Position 8–10 yards deep, favoring the L side.
  • Watch initial line charge for blocking fouls; be alert for illegal acts against the snapper.
  • Watch for Holding and pull-and-shoot action by B.

Try & Field Goal
  • Adjust depth (~5 yds), favoring the L side; watch defensive linemen over the snapper.
  • Watch for illegal action by Team B players not stationary within 1 yard of the LOS in a 3/4-point stance.
  • On snaps inside B-3: solely responsible for the ball passing over the crossbar.

Goal Line
  • Know the ineligibles; don't play as deep as on other scrimmage downs.
  • Hustle to the pile on a quick thrust into the line – make sure the ball isn't moved forward after being declared dead.
  • On snaps at/inside the 5, take primary responsibility for the passer beyond the neutral zone.
  • Never signal a score – assist H/L verbally or visually if needed.

Timeouts
  • Take charge of the ball; confirm timeouts remaining for each team with R.
  • Do not allow Team A to snap if Team B isn't ready (Delay of Game) after notification.

Close of Period / Half
  • Keep R informed of remaining time; advise if time expired during the down.
  • Transfer the ball to the new position at the end of each period.

Postgame
  • Leave the field with the crew; provide input for any incident report.

Head Linesman (H)

H runs the chain crew – the duties below fold in the Chain Crew's own procedures at the points where they matter to your position. The standalone Chain Crew page has the full detail if you're studying that role on its own.

Pregame
  • Organize the chain crew; locate the yardage chain and down marker.
  • Check the chain against the field's 10-yard measure; confirm the tape marker is at the exact midpoint.
  • Position assistants on the sideline opposite the press box – they move only on your signal.
  • Brief the crew: down box goes first at every spot; chain clip is set before the ball is snapped.

Coin Toss
  • After captains are escorted to midfield, take position at the 9-yard marks on your side.
  • Do not allow uninvolved team members inside the 9-yard marks.
  • Hold the game ball for the team on your side until the toss is decided.

Kickoff (Free Kicks)

The printed TASO manual's diagram and its own text paragraph disagree on this one – the diagram clearly shows H on the sideline opposite the press box (matching your own understanding); a text paragraph elsewhere in the manual says "press box side," which appears to be a manual typo. This guide follows the diagram.

  • Position: kicking team's restraining line, opposite the press box – the same sideline you hold the chains on for scrimmage downs (see the Kickoff Formation diagram on the Overview tab).
  • Count Team A (the kicking team) – on free kicks, U and H count the kicking team while R and L count the receiving team.
  • Confirm all non-players are clear of the restricted area.
  • Watch for Offside by Team B.
  • Once kicked, watch blocks ahead of the returner for Holding, Clipping, and Illegal Block Below the Waist.
  • Hold the goal line/pylon on your sideline until the ball is possessed.

Before the Snap (all scrimmage downs)
  • Read the offensive formation; know your eligible-receiver responsibilities.
  • Watch for Encroachment or a False Start by the offense.
  • Watch for Offside by the defense.
  • Take final position straddling the neutral zone, off the sideline.

Substitutions & Player Counts
  • You and L own the correct count on Team B, every down – see the Player Count Signal.
  • If Team A substitutes (no-huddle, or after breaking the huddle) on your sideline, give the Substitution Signal – that tells everyone Team B must get a chance to match.
  • Don't let the down get snapped until Team B has actually had that chance.

Running Plays
  • Watch blocking in front of the runner on your side for Holding, Clipping, Chop Block.
  • Mark forward progress on plays to your side; if the play goes out of bounds near you, move directly into the area.
  • If the play goes away from you, clean up behind R/U – especially late action on the QB after a backward pass.

Pass Plays
  • Help check the legality of interior offensive linemen's numbers.
  • Rule whether a pass is caught/touched at, behind, or beyond the neutral zone.
  • Watch for Offensive and Defensive Pass Interference on your side of the field.
  • Drift downfield cautiously the first 5–7 yards; watch for Ineligible Receiver Downfield.
  • On a quick screen near the line: rule forward vs. backward – give the Punch Back signal instead of the incomplete-pass signal if it went backward.

Scrimmage Kicks (Punts)
  • Responsible for the entire neutral zone at the snap; know if the kick crosses it.
  • Watch offensive backs and defenders in the line for Holding.
  • Watch gunners and close receivers for Kick-Catch Interference and valid/invalid fair-catch signals if the kick is short to your side.

Try & Field Goal
  • Take regular position on the sideline, observing the snap's legality and the whole neutral zone.
  • Snapped inside B-15: you are solely responsible for Roughing/Running Into the Kicker or Holder.

Goal Line
  • Sprint to the goal line at the snap when the ball is on/inside B's 5.
  • Turn your hips to look straight down the goal line – you must be on the line to rule on a score.
  • Punch the touchdown signal only when positive; otherwise mark forward progress and blow a crisp whistle.

Timeouts & Two-Minute
  • Notify the coach on your sideline of timeouts remaining and time left in the period.
  • At the Two-Minute Timeout (Varsity), join the on-field crew meeting, then relay the details to your head coach.

Close of Period / Half
  • Keep R informed of remaining time.
  • Between periods: reverse the chain crew's position to the opposite end of the field, following the standard chain-clip procedure.

Postgame
  • Leave the field with the crew; complete any incident report for fouls you called or witnessed.

Line Judge (L)

Pregame
  • Instruct the auxiliary box operator on duties and use of the line-to-gain indicator, if used.
  • Confirm game balls are available; brief Ball Assistants on their duties.

Coin Toss
  • Take position at the 9-yard marks on your side once captains are at midfield.
  • Hold the game ball for the team on your side until the toss is decided.

Kickoff (Free Kicks)
  • Position: goal line, press-box side (see the Kickoff Formation diagram on the Overview tab).
  • Count Team B players.
  • Ensure at least four players are on your side of the kicker when kicked.
  • Responsible for the goal line and pylon on your sideline until the ball is possessed.
  • Watch for Holding, Clipping, Illegal Block Below the Waist in front of the runner.

Before the Snap (all scrimmage downs)
  • Read the offensive formation; know your eligible-receiver responsibilities.
  • Watch for Encroachment or False Start by the offense, Offside by the defense.

Substitutions & Player Counts
  • You and H own the correct count on Team B, every down – see the Player Count Signal.
  • If Team A substitutes on your sideline, give the Substitution Signal the same way H does.

Running Plays
  • Same duties as H, mirrored on the press-box side – watch blocking for Holding/Clipping, mark forward progress on your side.

Pass Plays
  • Same duties as H, mirrored – rule catch/touch relative to the neutral zone, watch for interference and Ineligible Receiver Downfield.

Scrimmage Kicks (Punts)
  • Position outside and in front of the deep receiver(s); mark the end of the kick with your bean bag.
  • Primarily responsible for ruling on fair-catch signals in your area.
  • Watch for Kick-Catch Interference and batting violations.
  • On a quick screen/perimeter pass near the line: rule whether it actually went forward or backward – if backward, give the Punch Back signal, not the incomplete-pass signal.

Try & Field Goal
  • Position slightly behind the end line, center of the goal – sole responsibility for both uprights and crossbar (or per snap-distance variant, see manual).
  • Sound whistle when the ball becomes dead on the attempt.

Goal Line
  • Same as H – sprint to the goal line, rule on the score only when positive, mark forward progress otherwise.

Timeouts & Two-Minute
  • Confirm the game clock is stopped; time the timeout period.
  • Coordinate radio/TV breaks; signal when the timeout is over.

Close of Period / Half
  • Receive the ball from R at the close of the 2nd period; begin timing halftime once R starts the clock.
  • Sound three short whistle blasts when 20 seconds remain in the intermission.

Postgame
  • Leave the field with the crew; complete any incident report for fouls you called or witnessed.

Chain Crew / Clock Operator

Not an on-field official, but essential crew. Reports to H (the "Lines-person").

Pregame
  • Confirm attendance/arrival with H no later than two days before game day.
  • Arrive dressed, ready to take the field 30 minutes before kickoff.
  • Inspect all chain equipment for kinks, knots, weak spots; check the 10-yard measure against the field twice, using two different sets of 5-yard lines.
  • Confirm the chain is taped at its exact midpoint.
  • Clock operators: arrive 1 hour before kickoff, meet the officiating crew, review timing procedures and signals.

During the Game – Chains
  • Down box always goes first, at the forward-most point of the ball as indicated by H.
  • Back stake sets immediately behind the down box; line-to-gain stake pulls the chain taut, no kinks.
  • Chain clip is placed at the back side of the nearest 5-yard line in advance of the back stake, before the ball is next snapped.
  • Never move the down box, chains, or change the down number unless directed by H.
  • On a flag or possession change, hold position and inform H before moving – do not move on your own read of the play.

Measurements
  • Hold position until H signals you onto the field.
  • Line-to-gain stake holder hands the stake to the Umpire; H places the clip at the spot the Line Judge indicates.
  • Extend to full chain length only once H announces ready.

Clock Operator – Game Clock
  • Set to 12:00 at the start of every period.
  • Start on legal touching of a free kick, or on the Referee's start-the-clock signal, or on the snap if no signal is given.
  • Stop on: any charged timeout, after any score, ball out of bounds, a first down awarded, an incomplete pass, and the Two-Minute Timeout (Varsity only).
  • Keep a written log of every stoppage – if time erroneously runs off during a false start, correct it immediately without waiting for direction.

Clock Operator – Play Clock
  • Always starts at 40 seconds unless there's an administrative stoppage – then it's 25.
  • 25-second situations: penalty administration, start of a period, measurement, Team A helmet off/injury, Team B awarded a first down, after any kick down or score, timeout by either team or an official, touchback, try down, period extension, inadvertent whistle.
  • Referee resets manually: both palms overhead pumping = 40 seconds; one palm overhead pumping = 25 seconds.

Close of Period / Half
  • Reverse the chains to the opposite end of the field between the 1st and 3rd periods, following H's clip-and-reverse procedure.
  • Halftime intermission clock: 28:00 max (Varsity, regular season) unless otherwise agreed.

Pocket Card – Chain Crew / Clock Operator

Chain Crew Fundamentals

  • Down box first, every time.
  • Back stake immediately behind the down box; pull chain taut.
  • Chain clip always set before the next snap, at the back side of the nearest 5-yd line.
  • Never move without H's direction.
  • Flag or possession change seen? Hold and tell H – don't move.
  • Measurement: hold until waved on; extend only once H says ready.
  • Between 1st/3rd periods: reverse chains to the opposite end per H's clip-and-reverse procedure.

Clock Operator Fundamentals

  • Game clock: 12:00 at start of every period.
  • Stop on: any timeout, after any score, ball OOB, 1st down awarded, incomplete pass, Varsity 2-min timeout.
  • Play clock: always 40 unless an administrative stoppage sets it to 25.
  • 25-sec triggers: penalty, start of period, measurement, Team A helmet/injury, Team B 1st down, after kick/score, any timeout, touchback, try, period extension, inadvertent whistle.
  • Referee resets: both palms overhead = 40; one palm overhead = 25.
  • Log every stoppage; correct clock errors immediately, don't wait for direction.

Signal Glossary

Every hand signal and mechanic referenced anywhere in this guide, in one clickable place. Click any heading to expand it.

Player Count Signal (Fist / Open Palm)

What it is: A silent way for the two officials responsible for counting the same team to confirm they agree on the count, every down, without saying a word.

Who counts whom
  • Scrimmage downs: R and U count Team A; H and L count Team B.
  • Free kicks (kickoffs): the pairing flips – U and H count the kicking team, R and L count the receiving team.

The signal: closed fist = 11 players on the field (correct count). Open palm = fewer than 11.

Not part of TASO's illustrated signal chart – this is my own rendering of a crew-internal cue.

Substitution Signal

Official TASO Optional Signal O12 – "Team A substitution requiring Umpire to cover the ball"

What it is: The mechanic that guarantees the defense actually gets a chance to match an offensive substitution before the ball is snapped.

How it plays out, step by step
  • Team A substitutes (no-huddle, or after breaking the huddle): the wing official on Team A's sideline (H or L) signals – both arms held with closed fists, extended horizontal to the ground.
  • That tells everyone: "Team A is substituting, Team B must get the chance to do the same."
  • If it's clear Team B will respond with its own subs, R gives U the same signal.
  • U moves over the ball and prevents the snap until released (see the Release Signal).
  • The offense may not rush the snap specifically to deny the defense this opportunity – that's a foul (Illegal Substitution / Delay of Game) on the offense instead.
Release Signal

What it is: How R tells U the substitution process is over and it's safe to let the play continue.

The signal: R drops both arms and points at U. That's U's cue to release from covering the ball and return to a normal pre-snap position.

Not part of TASO's illustrated signal chart – this is my own rendering of a crew-internal cue.

Punch Back (Backward Pass Signal)

Official TASO Optional Signal O2 – "Backward pass (Punch back)"

What it is: The signal for "this pass actually went backward, not forward" – used instead of the incomplete-pass signal.

Why it matters: A backward (lateral) pass that hits the ground is a live ball (a fumble), not an incomplete pass. Giving the standard incomplete-pass signal on a pass that actually went backward tells everyone – players, chain crew, clock operator – the ball is dead when it's actually still live and recoverable.

The signal: a punching motion back toward your own end line.

When it comes up most: quick screens and shovel passes near the line, where forward-vs-backward is a genuine real-time judgment call – this is a duty for whichever official is ruling on that pass, most often L or H.

False Start

What it is: An offensive player moves – a flinch, a twitch, a hand coming up – after the ball is ready for play, in a way that simulates the start of the snap. The snapper shifting or moving the ball, a back jumping like the ball's already coming, and a lineman's quick jerky movement all count. It doesn't matter whether the defense reacts.

NCAA Rule (7-1-2-b)
"Each of the following is a false start by Team A if it occurs prior to the snap after the ball is ready for play and all players are in scrimmage formation: any movement by one or more players that simulates action at the snap... the snapper moving to another position... a restricted lineman moving their hand(s) or making any quick movement... an offensive player making any quick, jerky movement before the snap."
Exception: it is not a false start if a Team A lineman immediately reacts when threatened by a Team B player in the neutral zone. Penalty: dead-ball foul, 5 yards from the succeeding spot (not the previous spot – this is a pre-snap dead-ball foul, so it's marked off from where the ball was about to be snapped).
UIL Exception
None on this foul – enforced as written in the NCAA rule.
Varsity No tolerance given. Flag it every time.
JV If small, give a warning to the player/coach.
MS If egregious, flag it. If not, give the coach a chance to fix it.

Encroachment

What it is: An offensive player is in or beyond the neutral zone after the ball is ready for play, once the snapper has touched or simulated touching the ball, but before the snap.

NCAA Rule (Definitions, Sec. 18, Art. 1)
"After the ball is ready for play, encroachment occurs when an offensive player is in or beyond the neutral zone after the snapper touches or simulates touching the ball before the snap."
Exception: the snapper is not encroaching when in the neutral zone to put the ball in play. Penalty: dead-ball foul, 5 yards from the succeeding spot.
UIL Exception
None on this foul.
Varsity No tolerance given. Flag it every time.

Offside

What it is: There are actually two versions of this foul, with different enforcement:

The two variants
  • Live-ball Offside: a defensive player is in or beyond the neutral zone at the moment of the snap. Penalized like a normal live-ball foul.
  • Dead-ball Offside: before the snap, a defensive player crosses the neutral zone and continues charging toward a back without contact, causes an offensive lineman to react immediately, or otherwise commits a dead-ball offside act. This one is a pre-snap dead-ball foul, enforced the same way as False Start/Encroachment.
NCAA Rule (7-1-5-b, live-ball; 7-1-5-a, dead-ball)
"No player may be in or beyond the neutral zone at the snap... [dead-ball variant:] no player may cross the neutral zone and without making contact continue their charge toward any back."
Penalty (live-ball, at the snap): 5 yards from the previous spot. Penalty (dead-ball, before the snap): 5 yards from the succeeding spot.
UIL Exception
None on this foul.
Varsity No tolerance given. Flag it every time.
JV If small, give a warning to the player/coach. (inferred)
MS If egregious, flag it. If not, give the coach a chance to fix it. (inferred)
Holding (Offense) Judgment foul – not purely procedural

What it is: Using the hands or arms to grasp, pull, hook, clamp, or encircle an opponent in a way that illegally impedes them. Legal use of hands requires the hands in advance of the elbow, inside the frame of the opponent's body, at or below the shoulders.

NCAA Rule (9-3-3)
"The hand(s) and arm(s) shall not be used to grasp, pull, hook, clamp or encircle in any way that illegally impedes or illegally obstructs an opponent."
Penalty: 10 yards from the previous spot; Team A fouls behind their own goal line are a safety.
UIL Exception
None on this foul.
Varsity No tolerance given. Flag it every time. (inferred)

Clipping Safety foul – flag at every level

What it is: A block against an opponent where the force of the initial contact is from behind and at or below the waist. Not the same foul as Illegal Block Below the Waist – see that entry. The direction (from behind) is what makes it Clipping.

When it's actually legal
  • Against the ball carrier – the runner is never protected by this rule.
  • Going for a loose ball (fumble, backward pass, kick, touched forward pass) – may push an opponent low, at/toward the buttocks.
  • An eligible receiver behind the neutral zone, going for a forward pass, may push an opponent low at/to the buttocks.
  • The blocked player turned their back into a blocker who had already committed to that block's direction.
  • Inside the "blocking zone" (a rectangle centered on the middle offensive lineman, 5 yards to each side, 3 yards deep) – a lineman on the line at the snap may clip while the ball is still in the zone, but the contact must still be above the knee, and the block still can't violate Illegal Block Below the Waist.
NCAA Rule (9-1-5)
"There shall be no clipping. Exceptions: [blocking zone, turned back, going for a loose ball, going for a forward pass behind the neutral zone, against the runner]."
Penalty: 15 yards. Previous-spot enforcement for Team A fouls behind the neutral zone.
UIL Exception
None on this foul.
Varsity No tolerance given. Flag it every time.
JV No tolerance given. Flag it every time. (inferred – treated as a safety-adjacent contact foul like targeting/horse-collar; confirm.)
MS No tolerance given. Flag it every time. (inferred – confirm.)
Illegal Block Below the Waist Safety foul – flag at every level

What it is: A block below the waist that is not from behind (that's Clipping) – legality instead depends on who's blocking and the direction of contact. A tackle-box lineman or a stationary back may block below the waist inside the tackle box, but only if the force of contact is "directed from the front" (roughly 10 o'clock–2 o'clock relative to the player being blocked). Everyone else, and any block after a change of possession or during a kick (except against a runner), is illegal.

NCAA Rule (9-1-6)
"Linemen with initial position completely inside the tackle box may legally block below the waist inside the tackle box on their initial line charge... After the initial line charge, these linemen may block below the waist within the tackle box until the ball leaves the tackle box only if the force of the initial contact is directed from the front."
All other Team A players, and all Team B players outside the narrow tackle-box exception, may not block below the waist. Penalty: 15 yards, previous spot.
UIL Exception
UIL adds: for a lineman's position to count as "completely inside the tackle box," they must be within 5 yards of the snapper and no more than the second lineman from the snapper.
Varsity No tolerance given. Flag it every time.
JV No tolerance given. Flag it every time. (inferred.)
MS No tolerance given. Flag it every time. (inferred.)
Chop Block Safety foul – flag at every level

What it is: A high-low or low-high combination block by two players against the same opponent (not the ball carrier), anywhere on the field, with or without a delay between the two blocks – where the "low" component lands at the thigh or below.

When it's actually legal
  • Not a foul if the blocked player initiates the contact themselves.
  • Two simultaneous low blocks at the thigh (a "low-low" combination) are legal – the foul requires one high and one low component.
NCAA Rule (Definitions 2, Sec. 3, Art. 3)
"A chop block is a high-low or low-high combination block by any two players against an opponent (not the ball carrier) anywhere on the field, with or without a delay between blocks; the 'low' component is at the opponent's thigh or below. It is not a foul if the blockers' opponent initiates the contact."
Penalty: 15 yards, previous spot.
UIL Exception
None on this foul.
Varsity No tolerance given. Flag it every time.
JV No tolerance given. Flag it every time. (inferred.)
MS No tolerance given. Flag it every time. (inferred.)
Roughing the Passer / Running Into or Roughing the Kicker Safety foul – flag at every level

What it is (passer): Unnecessary rough contact with a passer once it's obvious the ball has been thrown – forcible head/neck contact, driving the passer to the ground and landing on them punitively, or any hit that was avoidable after the ball left their hand.

What it is (kicker/holder): See the flowchart on the Overview tab – Roughing (endangers the kicker) is 15 yards + auto first down; Running Into (just displaces them, no danger) is 5 yards with no auto first down.

NCAA Rule (9-1-9, Roughing the Passer)
"No defensive player shall unnecessarily rough a passer, when it is obvious the ball has been thrown."
Exception: a defender blocked into the passer with no opportunity to avoid contact is not at fault (though other personal fouls can still apply).
NCAA Rule (9-1-16, Kicker/Holder)
"When it is obvious that a scrimmage kick will be made, no opponent shall run into or rough the kicker or the holder of a place kick... When in question whether the foul is running into or roughing, the foul is roughing."
UIL Exception
None on either foul.
Varsity No tolerance given. Flag it every time.
JV No tolerance given. Flag it every time.
MS No tolerance given. Flag it every time.
Intentional Grounding

What it is: The passer, to conserve time or yardage, throws the ball forward into an area with no eligible Team A receiver – or throws it straight to the ground to stop the clock.

When it's actually legal
  • If the passer is (or has been) outside the tackle box and the ball crosses or lands beyond the neutral zone/neutral zone extended – this only applies to the player who controls the snap or resulting backward pass and hasn't given up the ball to a teammate first.
  • A legitimate spike to stop the clock, immediately after controlling the snap, is allowed under Rule 7-3-2-f.

Flagging this so whoever checks the guide knows exactly which line to double-check.

NCAA Rule (7-3-2-f, g, h)
"The passer to conserve time throws the ball directly to the ground... The passer to conserve time [or yardage] throws the ball forward into an area where there is no eligible Team A receiver."
Rulebook penalty text: loss of down at the spot of the foul. (See the verification note above.)

Illegal Forward Pass

What it is: A forward pass thrown from beyond the neutral zone, thrown by Team B, thrown after a change of possession during the down, a second forward pass on the same down, or thrown from behind the line after the runner has already carried the ball's entire body beyond the neutral zone.

NCAA Rule (7-3-2-a–e)
"A forward pass is illegal if it is thrown by a Team A player whose entire body is beyond the neutral zone when the ball is released... or it is the second forward pass during the same down."
Penalty: 5 yards from the spot of the foul, plus loss of down if by Team A before a change of possession.

Offensive Pass Interference

What it is: Contact by a Team A player beyond the neutral zone that interferes with a Team B player during a legal forward-pass play. It's the offensive player's job to avoid the defender, not the other way around.

When it's actually legal
  • An ineligible player immediately charges and contacts an opponent within 1 yard beyond the neutral zone, maintained no more than 3 yards beyond it.
  • Two or more eligible players making a simultaneous, bona fide attempt to reach/catch/bat the pass.
  • Two or more eligible players are in the area of a pass in flight and incidental impeding occurs, but the pass is not catchable.
NCAA Rule (7-3-8-b)
"Offensive pass interference is contact by a Team A player beyond the neutral zone that interferes with a Team B player during a legal forward pass play in which the forward pass crosses the neutral zone."
Penalty: 10 yards from the previous spot.
UIL Exception
None on this foul.

Defensive Pass Interference

What it is: Contact beyond the neutral zone by a Team B player whose intent to impede an eligible receiver is obvious, and which could prevent them from receiving a catchable pass. A catchable pass is assumed when in doubt.

When it's actually legal
  • Opposing players immediately charge and make contact within 1 yard beyond the neutral zone.
  • Two or more eligible players making a simultaneous, bona fide attempt on the ball.
  • Contact happened before the pass was thrown.
  • Contact during a fake scrimmage kick where the "kicker" throws the ball high and deep instead.
NCAA Rule (7-3-8-c)
"Defensive pass interference is contact beyond the neutral zone by a Team B player whose intent to impede an eligible opponent is obvious and could prevent the opponent the opportunity of receiving a catchable forward pass."
Penalty: spot of the foul, first down, if under 15 yards beyond the previous spot; 15 yards from the previous spot if 15+ yards downfield. Special rule: a foul that would otherwise be enforced outside the 2-yard line can never place the ball inside it – it instead goes to the 2, first down (or halfway to the goal line if the previous spot was already inside the 2).

Kick-Catch Interference

What it is: A receiving-team player attempting to catch a kick that has crossed the neutral zone must be given an unimpeded opportunity to do so. It's a foul if, before the receiver touches the ball, a Team A player enters the area of the receiver's shoulder width plus one yard in front of them.

When it's actually legal
  • Protection ends once the kick touches the ground, or Team B muffs/touches it beyond the neutral zone (with fair-catch-signal exceptions).
  • Not a foul if the interference resulted from the receiver being legally blocked by an opponent.

"If an inbounds player of the receiving team is located where they could catch a free kick or a scrimmage kick that has crossed the neutral zone, and if the player is attempting to do so, they must be given an unimpeded opportunity to catch the kick."

Targeting Safety foul – flag at every level, no exceptions

What it is: A player takes aim at an opponent to attack with forcible contact that goes beyond a legal tackle, block, or play on the ball – leading with the helmet, forearm, fist, or shoulder at the head/neck area, or lowering the head to strike with the crown of the helmet. Launching or crouching-then-thrusting upward into an opponent's head/neck both count. When in question, it's a foul.

"'Targeting' means that a player takes aim at an opponent for purposes of attacking with forcible contact that goes beyond making a legal tackle or a legal block or playing the ball."

What it is: The play clock reaches :00 before the ball is snapped, or the offense/defense isn't ready to play after an intermission, score, or timeout. Also includes deliberately advancing a dead ball.

Illegal Substitution

What it is: This name actually covers several distinct violations, not one foul:

The different ways it happens
  • An incoming substitute enters the field (or an end zone) while the ball is live, or more than 11 players leave the field while the ball is live.
  • A substitute-turned-player, or a replaced player, doesn't stay in/out for at least one full play (with the usual exceptions: between periods, after a score, or a charged timeout).
  • The offense snaps the ball without giving the defense a real chance to match a substitution – officials hold the snap until Team B has responded, and won't let Team A rush the play specifically to deny that chance.
  • Either team has more than 11 players in the huddle/formation for more than 3 seconds, or more than 11 on the field at the snap.
  • A substitution used to deceive as part of a trick play (e.g., a play that can only work by hiding a player near the bench).
NCAA Rule (3-5-2, 3-5-3)
"When Team A sends in its substitutes, the officials will not allow the ball to be snapped until Team B has been given an opportunity to substitute... Team A is prohibited from rushing quickly to the line of scrimmage with the obvious attempt of creating a defensive disadvantage."
Penalties vary by which violation occurred: entering/leaving illegally or not playing a full down is 5 yards; failing to let the defense match is a Delay of Game (5 yards, succeeding spot) charged to whichever team caused it; too many players in a formation/huddle is 5 yards (dead-ball foul for Team A, live-ball foul for Team B). After the Two-Minute Timeout, a 12-men-on-the-field defensive foul can also reset the game clock to the time at the snap, at the offense's option.
UIL Exception
None on this foul.
Varsity No tolerance given. Flag it every time. (inferred.)
JV If small/borderline (a player a step slow leaving), give a warning once. (inferred.)
MS Coach it first (this is often a bench-management mistake, not deception) unless it's a repeated or clearly deceptive tactic. (inferred.)
